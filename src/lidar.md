## What is LIDAR?
	Ah yes, the age old question from budding code-enthusiasts. LIDAR is a way to map out a robot’s surroundings by shooting pulses of light and timing how long it takes until they are reflected back to the robot, letting you calculate the distance from the sensor to the surface. Because we don’t have infinite sensors, to get a 3d effect the sensor spins around the vertical axis and a little around the pitch axis to get data from different angles. This results in a “point cloud”, containing a bunch of 3d points relative to the sensor. These generally form the shape of the surroundings, and this point cloud can be cross-referenced with a 3d model of the field to produce accurate localization results. Also, where LIDAR really shines is in its ability to detect the positions of other robots and game elements, as obviously those don’t have april tags on them. This is very useful for autos, since it can let us, for example, move toward dynamically changing clumps of balls to more efficiently intake them, or avoid other robots while executing our autonomous routine. Additionally, LIDAR works anywhere on the field while limelights are limited to wherever you can see april tags, so LIDAR is useful in those spots for more accurate localization. To actually get the robot’s position from LIDAR point cloud data, you can use Adaptive Monte Carlo Localization or AMCL for short.
